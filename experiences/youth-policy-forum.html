Field + FacilitationResearch, visits and session work

Evidence for Accountability: Khulna

As part of an FCDO-supported Youth Policy Forum project, I participated in two days of field research in Khulna examining how river encroachment affects the social, economic, and health conditions of riverside communities. The fieldwork covered both the urban Rupsha River area and the rural Shoilmari River, where we engaged with people from different occupations and backgrounds. As my first field research experience, it provided valuable exposure to grassroots perspectives and demonstrated how closely rivers, livelihoods, and community life are connected.

Evidence for Accountability: Buriganga

The Buriganga field visit formed part of the same FCDO-supported research on the effects of river encroachment on surrounding communities. Compared with the earlier work in Khulna, respondents in Dhaka were noticeably more cautious about sharing information, being photographed, or discussing issues that could affect their livelihoods. This experience highlighted the importance of trust, sensitivity, and adaptability in field research, particularly when working in contexts where participants may perceive social, economic, or political risks in speaking openly.

Evidence for Accountability: Panchagarh

As part of the Evidence for Accountability project with Youth Policy Forum, I participated in field research in Panchagarh examining how river erosion, stone extraction, livelihoods, environmental change, and accountability intersect in local communities. We spoke with residents, stone extraction workers, and students to understand how these issues are experienced in everyday life. The fieldwork highlighted the complexity of local realities: while changing river conditions and extraction activities can create environmental and social pressures, they also remain important sources of income for many people. The experience reinforced the importance of understanding policy and accountability issues through the perspectives of those directly affected by them.

Field Visit to Dhaka Polytechnic Institute

As part of Youth Policy Forum’s Education & Skills Policy Team, I participated in a field visit to Dhaka Polytechnic Institute for an ongoing study of Bangladesh’s Technical and Vocational Education and Training (TVET) system. Discussions with institutional leadership, faculty members, and students provided firsthand insight into the diploma structure, institutional challenges, practical training, and initiatives supporting skills development and employability. The visit helped connect policy-level analysis with the realities of technical education institutions and reinforced the importance of stronger links between classroom learning, practical training, and industry.

Training for facilitation

I participated in the two-day Youth Led Futures Masterclass organized by Youth Policy Forum, focusing on local governance, youth participation, civic engagement, and the role of young people in policy and decision-making processes. The sessions combined policy concepts with practical community contexts, strengthening my understanding of youth engagement and providing useful preparation for subsequent facilitation and community-focused activities.
